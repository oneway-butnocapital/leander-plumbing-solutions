Sewer Line Repair in Leander, TX

Camera-led sewer line checks and repairs across Leander and nearby communities.

Call (512) 698-2730 Ask a question

Older homes around Leander, Cedar Park, Jonestown, and Liberty Hill can have a sewer lateral with clay tile joints, cast-iron sections, or older fiber pipe below the yard. Those materials can develop open joints, root entry, corrosion, bellies, and cracks as soil shifts around the pipe. A newer house can have sewer trouble as well when construction debris, grease, wipes, or a damaged fitting restricts the line between the fixtures and the main connection.

We start by separating a single fixture issue from a problem farther down the sewer line. When a toilet, shower, tub, or floor drain reacts every time another fixture drains, the shared pipe is often the place to investigate. The outdoor cleanout, the lowest drain in the house, and any wet area over the buried route can all provide useful evidence before excavation is considered.

A sewer line fault should not wait when wastewater is backing up, a cleanout is overflowing, or multiple fixtures are draining into each other. A partial blockage can become a full stoppage, while a broken underground pipe can allow wastewater to escape into soil or return through a shower, tub, or floor drain.

Gurgling toilets and yard odors

Customers often call us because the toilet gurgles when the washing machine empties, the shower fills with dirty water, or the tub drains slowly after the sink is used. Another common report is a bubbling toilet bowl, especially when a nearby fixture sends water down the drain. These signs can point to a restriction in a shared branch line or farther out in the sewer lateral.

A sewage smell near the outdoor cleanout, a damp patch above the pipe route, or water standing around a cleanout cap can indicate a separate issue. Repeated backups at the lowest shower, tub, or floor drain may come from roots, an offset pipe joint, a crushed section, or a blockage inside the line. The symptoms can look similar from inside the house, but the fault behind them and the repair scope can be very different once we inspect the pipe.

Camera inspection and line test

On a sewer line inspection call, we locate the available cleanout and check which fixtures are affected. We run water where appropriate, look for standing wastewater at the cleanout, and use a sewer camera to view the inside of the pipe when access allows. The camera can show root masses, separated joints, scaling, broken pipe walls, and the point where water stops moving. Many residential sewer lines use pipe around 3 or 4 inches in diameter, and the camera head helps us identify whether the restriction is in that main run or closer to one fixture.

A basic cleanout check and camera pass can often be completed within 30 to 60 minutes, although a buried cleanout, a full stoppage, or a long pipe route can take longer. We note the pipe material, the location of the defect, and the depth reading from the camera transmitter when conditions allow. An accessible cleanout cap, short exposed pipe section, or minor connection repair may be handled on the visit; replacement beneath a driveway, slab, landscaping, or deep yard area is normally planned after utility locating, access planning, and any required permit or sign-off.

Pipe material, depth, and access

The pipe material changes the repair approach. Clay pipe can separate at joints, cast iron can corrode from the inside, and older fiber pipe can deform or deteriorate along its length. A localized break in an exposed section is a different job from a long run of failing pipe, repeated root entry at several joints, or a sewer lateral with damage beneath a concrete driveway.

Access also affects the scope of the work. An outdoor cleanout that opens, a clear route through a side yard, and a visible repair point simplify inspection and replacement. A seized cleanout cap, buried pipe beneath a slab, utility lines near the route, landscaping, retaining walls, or a connection that needs municipal sign-off adds planning, labor, and materials. If a specific fitting, cleanout assembly, or replacement section must be ordered, we explain that before work is scheduled.

Cleanout location and pipe material

Before you call, find the outdoor cleanout if you can and note whether the cap is near the house, driveway, fence, or side yard. Tell us which room and fixture first showed the backup, such as a downstairs shower, hall toilet, laundry drain, or floor drain. If you have construction records or a previous repair invoice, the pipe material and prior camera notes can help identify the sewer route. Let us know whether wastewater is standing at the cleanout, coming through a drain, or only causing a gurgling toilet.
