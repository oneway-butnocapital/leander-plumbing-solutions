Drain Cleaning in Leander, TX

We clear slow, clogged, gurgling, and odorous drains across Leander.

Call (512) 698-2730 Ask a question

A sink that gurgles, a shower drain that holds water, or a sewer-like smell from a bathroom floor drain usually means wastewater is not moving through the pipe as it should. We trace the symptom back from the fixture opening, trap, and branch line rather than treating every slow drain as the same blockage.

Grease in a kitchen line, hair around a tub stopper, soap buildup in a shower trap, and paper caught farther along a toilet branch can all make water rise or drain slowly. A plunger may shift a soft blockage near the fixture, but repeated plunging can leave the material farther down the pipe and make the next backup harder to clear.

Drain cleaning should not be put off when water is rising in a basin, coming up through another fixture, or leaving wastewater on a floor. A restricted drain can progress from a slow fixture to an overflow because every use adds more water and debris behind the blockage.

Gurgling sinks and drain smells

We often hear that a kitchen sink makes a gulping sound after the faucet is turned off, a shower fills around the ankles, or a bathroom sink drains only after the water has sat in the bowl. Other callers notice a rotten or sewer-like smell near a floor drain, a washing machine standpipe that spills, or water appearing in a nearby tub when another fixture is emptied.

A gurgle can point to restricted flow or a venting issue, while an odor may come from a dry trap, decaying material in a pipe, or wastewater held behind a blockage. Water in the wrong fixture can indicate a restriction farther down the shared branch. These symptoms can look similar from the room, but the pipe route, blockage location, and work needed to correct them can be very different.

Drain line inspection

At the visit, we start with the affected fixture, run water where appropriate, and check whether nearby sinks, tubs, or floor drains react. We inspect the stopper, strainer, trap, trap arm, cleanout, and visible pipe joints for buildup, leaks, or signs that water has been standing. A straightforward fixture and trap check often takes about 15 to 30 minutes, while tracing a shared branch line can take longer when access is through a cabinet, wall, crawlspace, or exterior cleanout.

We identify the pipe material and opening size before choosing a cable, auger, or other clearing method. Many bathroom sink drains use a 1 1/4-inch or 1 1/2-inch trap, while kitchen and larger branch lines may use wider pipe. We can usually clear accessible buildup, remove hair from a stopper, clean a trap, and test drainage during the same visit. If we find a damaged trap arm, failed cleanout plug, collapsed line, or pipe section that needs replacement, that repair may need separate scheduling after the affected section and access route are confirmed.

Pipe route and cleanout access

The pipe route and the available access change the scope of a drain job. A blockage reached through a cabinet trap or an exterior cleanout is different from one that requires moving a washing machine, opening a crawlspace hatch, or working through a tight vanity. A cleanout cap that is seized, damaged, buried, or painted over can add preparation before we can reach the line.

Pipe material and the condition of the branch also matter. Older cast iron can hold scale on its inner wall, while plastic pipe may collect grease, soap, or foreign objects at fittings and bends. A blockage close to the fixture is generally easier to locate than one farther along a shared line with multiple turns. If a camera inspection, pipe replacement, or building approval is needed after clearing attempts, we explain that added work and the reason before it is arranged.

Fixture location and cleanout access

Before calling, we ask that you note which room and fixture is affected, such as the kitchen sink, upstairs shower, laundry standpipe, or garage floor drain. Tell us whether water is standing in the basin now, whether another sink or tub reacts when you run water, and whether the issue began after grease, food scraps, wipes, or another item entered the drain. If you know the pipe material or the approximate age of the home plumbing, that can help us plan for the trap and branch line. Let us know if an exterior cleanout is visible and whether the route to it is blocked by a gate, landscaping, or stored equipment.
