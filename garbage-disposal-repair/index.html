Garbage Disposal Repair in Leander, TX

Help for humming, jammed, leaking, or non-working garbage disposals.

Call (512) 698-2730 Ask a question

Most calls begin with a kitchen sink that “just hums,” a switch that does nothing, or water that will not leave the bowl. We also hear that the disposal “stopped chopping,” “smells bad,” or has started leaking into the cabinet. Those are the words people use at the sink, and they can point to a jammed grinding plate, a tripped reset, a failed motor, a loose mounting ring, or a blocked drain connection.

We check the disposal unit, switch, outlet, reset button, splash guard, drain trap, and the cabinet floor before deciding whether the problem is in the appliance or farther along the waste pipe. A humming motor needs a different repair from a disposal that has no power, and a leak at the sink flange is different from water coming back through the drain.

A disposal that simply will not run can often wait if the sink is kept out of use and no water is leaking. Water under the cabinet, sewage-like odor, repeated breaker trips, or a sink that cannot drain should not wait, because standing water can damage the cabinet base and a strained motor can fail completely.

Humming motors and sink water

A low hum with no grinding usually means the motor has power but the grinding plate cannot turn. Food debris, a utensil, a small bone, or a damaged internal component can lock the plate in place. A unit that clicks or goes silent after the wall switch is used may have a tripped reset, a failed switch connection, a loose cord, or a motor that has stopped operating.

Other calls involve water filling the sink while the disposal runs, a sour odor coming from the chamber, water dripping from the bottom of the unit, or a disposal that grinds but leaves food in the bowl. Water at the cabinet floor can come from the dishwasher connection, drain elbow, sink flange, or disposal housing. These symptoms can sound alike from the kitchen, but the fault behind them and the repair cost can be very different once we inspect the unit.

Disposer callout test

During the callout, we isolate power at the switch and breaker, check the reset button, and test the outlet and cord for a standard residential 120-volt supply. We inspect the grinding chamber with a light, turn the plate with the service wrench where the unit has a wrench socket, and check the splash guard, mounting assembly, dishwasher inlet, drain elbow, and trap. An initial electrical and mechanical check generally takes about 20 to 40 minutes, depending on cabinet access and the condition of the fittings.

If the problem is a recoverable jam, loose drain connection, reset issue, worn splash guard, or accessible mounting problem, we can usually complete that work during the same visit after testing the sink for leaks and drainage. Replacing a disposal commonly adds about 45 to 90 minutes because the old unit, mounting ring, discharge tube, and electrical connection need to be removed and fitted correctly. A replacement unit, unusual mounting hardware, or a model-specific part that must be ordered is booked for a return visit.

Motor age and cabinet access

The age and model of the disposal matter because an older sealed motor with a failed internal winding is generally a replacement situation, while an accessible jam or reset may only need testing and clearing. The model plate also affects whether a matching splash guard, mounting ring, discharge tube, or replacement unit is readily available. A unit with corrosion around the mounting collar or cracks in the housing can require more work than a disposal that has simply been knocked out of position.

Cabinet access changes the scope as well. Cleaning supplies, shelving, tight pipework, and a disposal mounted close to the cabinet wall can make removal and leak testing more involved. If work at the sink flange or dishwasher connection exposes a seized shutoff, damaged drain fitting, or electrical connection that needs code-related sign-off, we explain the added work before moving ahead.

Model plate and sink cabinet

Before calling, look for the model plate on the side or underside of the disposal housing inside the sink cabinet. The make and model help us identify the mounting system, cord connection, and likely replacement parts. Let us know which kitchen sink has the problem and whether the unit is humming, running, leaking, or completely silent. If water is present, check whether it is coming from the bottom of the disposal, the drain elbow, or the sink opening above it. Clear enough space around the cabinet door for access, but do not put a hand or tool into the grinding chamber.
